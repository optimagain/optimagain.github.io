10 Foods, Herbs and Spices Studied for Blood Sugar Support

Food first, movement next, sleep and stress always — then this layer. Here are three of the ten, with the amounts actually used in research.

  1. Cinnamon (Ceylon)Studied for its effect on how the body responds to insulin. Ceylon rather than Cassia matters at daily amounts, because Cassia carries coumarin. Stir it into oatmeal, yoghurt or coffee.
  2. MagnesiumA cofactor in normal insulin function, and one of the more commonly under-consumed minerals. Pumpkin seeds, spinach, almonds and black beans are all solid food sources.
  3. Alpha-lipoic acidAn antioxidant studied in the context of insulin sensitivity and nerve health. Found in small amounts in spinach and broccoli.

The order matters more than the list

This is a supporting layer, not a foundation. Meals, movement, sleep and stress do the structural work; everything on this list sits on top of that.

Anyone selling you the reverse has the order backwards, and it is worth being suspicious of them.

Read the “avoid if” column before anything else

Several of these interact meaningfully with common medications, and a few are not appropriate during pregnancy or with kidney or liver conditions.

The reference card includes those notes for all ten. Please talk to your doctor or pharmacist before adding any of them, particularly if you already take something for blood sugar — the interaction risk is real and specific.
